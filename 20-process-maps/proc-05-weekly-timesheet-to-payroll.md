# Weekly Timesheet to Payroll and Invoicing

## Process Overview

- **Purpose:** Turn each week's worked hours into accurate pay for workers and accurate invoices for clients.
- **Trigger:** The work week ends.
- **End condition:**
  - **Phase 1:** workers are paid through the bank file generated by Phlo, the amounts are recorded in QuickBooks accounts, and each client's billable hours are ready for invoicing.
  - **Phase 2:** clients are invoiced from Phlo.
- **Frequency:** Weekly, for every worker at every client. Payroll and invoicing both run weekly.
- **Typical duration:** The timesheet arrives Monday about 8 AM. [UNKNOWN: time to payroll]

## Current Process (as-is)

1. A timekeeper at each client site manages punch-ins and punch-outs every day. Workers initial each day; the supervisor signs each page.
2. At the end of the week, the timesheet arrives at Friendly Staffing as a PDF. Example: 17 pages, about 115 workers, Molinaro's, week of 2026-08-10.
3. Staff read the handwritten sheets and enter each worker's regular and overtime hours (above 44 hours) into the **payroll Excel**.
   - The Excel is a database of workers grouped under employer names.
   - It holds each worker's rate for the job, and the worker's payment method.
4. The Excel calculates each worker's **gross pay**: regular hours × regular rate, plus overtime hours × overtime rate.
5. Gross pay is processed by payment method:
   - **Method A, direct deduction:** the worker is treated as an employee. Government deductions are calculated in the Excel.
   - **Method B, subcontractor:** no deductions.
   - A worker who chose both has regular and overtime hours split between the two methods. The split does not change week to week.
6. The result is a **final pay figure** per worker and payment method.
7. Staff create a **pay stub in QuickBooks** for each worker: under the employer, the worker's name, hours, payment type (A or B) and final pay. QuickBooks verifies the deductions. Staff record the transaction.
8. Staff export an **XML payment file** from QuickBooks and import it into the **bank portal**, which pays the workers. Every worker, on either method, is paid through the bank.
9. Staff use the same payroll Excel to prepare **client invoices**. With few clients, each invoice is recorded manually in QuickBooks, generated and sent to the employer, who pays it.

## Phase 1 Process (to-be)

Decision 2026-11-03 (Tejash's recommendation, confirmed by Rohan): payroll finalisation, pay stubs, the bank XML file and Friendly Staffing's own timekeeping move into Phase 1. Phlo replaces the payroll Excel and QuickBooks payroll. QuickBooks stays for accounts only. Timekeeping is a separate module so it can be re-phased later if needed.

1. Hours are captured for the week:
   - **Phlo timekeeping module:** the timekeeper marks attendance on her phone: present or absent, punches in and out, and breaks. Workers sign for their hours on their own phones. No tablets. [UNKNOWN: the detailed attendance workflow is still to be designed]
   - **Sites not yet on the module:** the timekeeper sends the signed weekly timesheet and staff enter the times in Phlo against the assignments.
2. Phlo checks the hours:
   - worked hours match a confirmed assignment
   - break rules for the client and position, at or above the ESA eating-period minimum
   - overtime after 44 hours, counted across **all clients** the worker worked for that week
   - minimum pay rules, e.g. the three-hour rule when a shift is cut short
   - study-permit weekly hour cap
   - pay rate at or above Ontario minimum wage ($17.95/hr from 2026-10-01)
3. Phlo flags any exceptions.
   - **If exceptions:** go to Exception A.
   - **If none:** go to step 4.
4. The employer confirms the week's hours. [UNKNOWN: in Phlo, or by signing the summary as today]
5. Phlo calculates gross pay from the **pay rate**: regular hours × regular rate, plus overtime hours × overtime rate.
6. Phlo splits each worker's hours by payment method. A worker on both methods has regular and overtime hours split by the worker's set split. The split stays the same week to week; staff can change it when the worker asks.
7. Phlo calculates final pay:
   - **Method A:** CPP, EI and income tax (using the worker's TD1 and TD1ON). Vacation pay is added to each weekly pay [to confirm with Tejash], plus public holiday pay.
   - **Method B:** no deductions.
8. Staff review the payroll run and finalise it.
9. Phlo produces each worker's **pay stub**, showing hours, rates, gross, deductions and net by payment method.
10. Phlo produces the **XML payment file**. Staff upload it to the bank portal, which pays every worker. No workers are paid outside the bank file.
11. Phlo pushes the week's final amounts to **QuickBooks accounts**, which records the transactions.
12. Phlo produces each client's **billable hours summary** from the **bill rate**: hours and amounts, regular and overtime. Staff use it to record the invoice in QuickBooks. The bill rate is agreed separately with each client and is independent of the pay rate.
13. Phlo sends the hours summary to the employer, and the pay stub to each worker.
14. Phlo updates the margin report: (bill rate − pay rate) × hours by worker, order, client and week, before employer costs.

### Exception A: Hours Do Not Check

A1. Staff review the flagged line: missing punch, hours without an assignment, a break below the minimum, or a study-permit cap breach.
A2. Staff resolve it with the employer. The conversation is logged.
A3. Staff correct the entry. Return to step 2.

### Statutory Figures (Phase 1, decided 2026-11-03)

Phlo calculates and produces these. The payments to and from the government are made outside Phlo, by Friendly Staffing.
- CRA remittance amounts (employee deductions plus employer CPP and EI) for each remittance period
- T4 slips at year end, combining QuickBooks year-to-date history with Phlo
- ROEs, triggered by 7-day interruptions of earnings
- WSIB premium figures

### Cut-over

- QuickBooks year-to-date payroll history is imported.
- One payroll cycle runs in parallel (Phlo and the Excel/QuickBooks process) before Phlo takes over.

## Phase 2 Changes

1. **Invoicing:** weekly invoices from approved billable hours × bill rate, plus 13% HST, plus conversion fees. Receivables are tracked and reminders sent. How bill rates are derived is still to be understood.
2. **Reporting:** loaded margin after employer costs, and profitability by client. Employer costs differ between method A and method B workers.

## Known Issues

| Issue | Impact | Current Workaround |
|---|---|---|
| Handwritten timesheets | Slow, error-prone re-keying; OCR unreliable | Manual reading |
| Hours in hours:minutes | Conversion errors | Manual conversion |
| Hours keyed into Excel, then re-keyed into QuickBooks stubs and invoices | Double entry, mismatches between pay and billing | Manual checks |
| QuickBooks costs about CAD 8,000 a year for payroll and accounts, with per-employee pricing planned | Unsuited to high-turnover staffing | Phase 1: payroll moves to Phlo; QuickBooks kept for accounts |
| Hours disputes | Rework, worker complaints | Worker initials per day |
